## Whose Voice Survives the Hero's Story?
英雄故事结束后，谁的声音能够留下？

## Today Card｜首页卡片版

In *The Penelopiad*, Margaret Atwood retells *The Odyssey* from the underworld. Penelope finally speaks after centuries of being remembered as the faithful wife who waited, wove, and endured. But she is not the only one who returns. The twelve maids hanged after Odysseus's homecoming interrupt her story with songs, accusations, jokes, and demands for justice.

在《珀涅罗珀记》中，玛格丽特·阿特伍德让《奥德赛》的故事从冥界重新开始。几个世纪以来，珀涅罗珀一直被记作那个忠贞等待、不断织布并默默忍耐的妻子；如今，她终于获得讲述自己的机会。但回来的并不只有她。奥德修斯返乡后被绞死的十二名女仆，以歌唱、控诉、讽刺与对正义的要求，不断打断她的故事。

Penelope revises the legend that turned her into a model for other women. Yet the maids also question her version. They were enslaved girls who worked, carried messages, endured sexual coercion, and helped protect their mistress's household. Their chorus asks whether giving one privileged woman a voice is enough when poorer women remain unnamed.

珀涅罗珀修订了那个把她塑造成女性典范的传说，但女仆们也在质疑她的版本。她们是被奴役的女孩，承担劳动、传递消息、遭受性胁迫，也曾帮助女主人保护家宅。她们的合唱追问：如果较贫穷的女性仍然没有名字，那么只让一位拥有特权的女性发声，真的足够吗？

Atwood's feminist rewriting does not simply replace a male hero with a flawless female heroine. It asks who owns a story, whose labour supports the household, whose suffering becomes a symbol, and whose death can be excused as the custom of another age.

阿特伍德的女性主义重写，并不是用一位完美女主人公替换男性英雄。它追问谁拥有故事，谁的劳动支撑家庭，谁的痛苦被转换成象征，又是谁的死亡可以被“另一个时代的习俗”轻易解释过去。

> “we had no voice / we had no name / we had no choice”

> “我们没有声音 / 没有名字 / 没有选择。”

— Margaret Atwood, *The Penelopiad*

## A Myth Retold from Hades
一个从冥界重新讲述的神话

*The Penelopiad* was published in 2005 as part of the Canongate Myths series. Atwood returns to the story of Odysseus, Penelope, and the twelve maids who are hanged near the end of Homer's *Odyssey*. Her book shifts narrative authority away from the returning warrior and gives it to two kinds of dead women: a queen remembered too neatly by tradition and enslaved servants whom epic barely allows to speak.

《珀涅罗珀记》于2005年作为 Canongate 神话重写系列的一部分出版。阿特伍德重新进入奥德修斯、珀涅罗珀与十二名女仆的故事；这些女仆在荷马《奥德赛》接近结尾时被绞死。她把叙事权从返乡的战士手中移开，交给两种已经死去的女性：一位被传统过度整齐地记忆的王后，以及一群在史诗中几乎没有发言机会的被奴役者。

Penelope narrates in prose from Hades. The maids appear between her chapters as a chorus, changing form each time: lament, rope-jumping rhyme, ballad, sea shanty, drama, anthropology lecture, trial transcript, and love song. This formal instability matters. Penelope tries to make a continuous account of her life; the maids refuse to remain supporting characters inside that account.

珀涅罗珀在冥界以散文讲述自己。女仆们则作为合唱队出现在她的章节之间，每一次都更换形式：哀歌、跳绳童谣、民谣、船歌、戏剧、 anthropology lecture（人类学讲座）、审判记录与情歌。这种形式上的不稳定十分重要。珀涅罗珀试图建立一份连续的人生叙述，女仆们却拒绝继续充当这份叙述里的配角。

## Penelope Takes Up the Thread
珀涅罗珀拿起自己的线

In the traditional story, Penelope is famous for waiting. During Odysseus's twenty-year absence, she delays remarriage by promising to choose a suitor after completing a burial shroud for Laertes. She weaves by day and secretly unweaves by night. Atwood preserves this act of cunning but connects textile work directly to storytelling: Penelope now wants to spin a thread of her own.

在传统故事中，珀涅罗珀以等待闻名。奥德修斯离家二十年间，她承诺在为拉厄耳忒斯织完裹尸布后选择一位求婚者；她白天织布，夜晚秘密拆除。阿特伍德保留了这一机智行动，同时把纺织劳动直接连接到叙事：现在，珀涅罗珀要纺出一根属于自己的线。

Her account corrects the simplifications of heroic myth. Odysseus's adventures become competing rumours rather than unquestioned truth. Penelope's waiting is revealed as household management, political calculation, information gathering, and survival. She is not a still figure at the edge of a man's voyage. She is governing an unstable estate while trying to prevent violence and forced remarriage.

她的叙述修正了英雄神话的简化。奥德修斯的冒险不再是无需质疑的真相，而变成彼此竞争的传闻。珀涅罗珀的等待也显露为家宅管理、政治计算、情报搜集与生存策略。她并不是静止在男人旅程边缘的人；她在管理一个不稳定的庄园，同时设法阻止暴力与强迫再婚。

## The Faithful Wife as a Disciplinary Myth
作为规训神话的忠贞妻子

Penelope has been praised for fidelity, intelligence, patience, and domestic order. In Atwood's version, that praise is not harmless. Once she becomes the exemplary wife, her story can be used to judge other women: endure more, complain less, remain sexually faithful, and preserve the household whatever the cost.

珀涅罗珀因忠贞、智慧、耐心与维持家庭秩序而受到赞美。在阿特伍德的版本里，这种赞美并非无害。一旦她成为模范妻子，她的故事便可以被用来评判其他女性：要求她们忍耐更多、抱怨更少、保持性忠贞，并且不惜代价维护家庭。

The book therefore distinguishes representation from liberation. A woman can occupy a celebrated place in cultural memory while the image attached to her continues to discipline women living under very different conditions. Penelope's desire to retell the myth is partly a desire to escape the uses to which her reputation has been put.

因此，这本书区分了“获得再现”与“获得解放”。一个女人可以在文化记忆中拥有备受赞美的位置，而附着于她的形象仍然会规训生活在完全不同条件下的女性。珀涅罗珀重新讲述神话的愿望，也包含着摆脱后人对她名声之利用的愿望。

## The Chorus Refuses a Single Heroine
合唱队拒绝只有一个女主人公

If the book contained only Penelope's voice, it could become a familiar recovery story: a misunderstood woman finally explains herself. The maids prevent that resolution. They repeatedly return to the same fact that Penelope's elegant narrative cannot absorb: twelve girls died, and the household's rulers survived.

如果书中只有珀涅罗珀的声音，它可能会成为一种熟悉的“找回女性”故事：一位遭到误解的女人终于解释了自己。女仆们阻止了这种圆满。她们不断回到珀涅罗珀优雅叙述无法吸收的事实：十二个女孩死了，而家宅的统治者活了下来。

Their collective voice also resists the demand for individual character development. Most of the maids are not given separate names or complete biographies. This absence repeats the historical violence done to them, but their chorus turns anonymity into coordinated speech. They speak as those whose differences were erased by labour, sexual availability, punishment, and the manner of their death.

她们的集体声音也拒绝“每个人都必须拥有完整个人成长线”的要求。大多数女仆没有独立姓名，也没有完整传记。这种缺席重复了历史施加给她们的暴力，但合唱队把匿名转化成协调一致的发言。她们作为一群人说话，因为劳动、被迫承受性支配、惩罚与死亡方式抹去了她们之间的差异。

## Almost Like Sisters, Never Equals
几乎像姐妹，却从不平等

Penelope remembers the twelve maids as girls she had acquired and trained inside the household. They help her unweave the shroud and gather information from the suitors. During the secret night work, distinctions of rank appear to soften; mistress and servants laugh together and seem, in Penelope's phrase, almost like sisters.

珀涅罗珀回忆说，十二名女仆是她得到并在家中训练的女孩。她们帮助拆除裹尸布，也从求婚者那里搜集消息。在秘密的夜间劳动中，等级差异似乎有所松动；女主人与仆人一起说笑，用珀涅罗珀的话说，她们几乎像姐妹。

The word “almost” carries the argument. Penelope can experience intimacy without surrendering ownership or authority. She assigns the maids dangerous work, asking them to flirt with the suitors and spy on them, but keeps this plan secret from Eurycleia. The maids' apparent disloyalty is therefore partly a role performed under Penelope's instruction, while the risks of that performance fall on their bodies.

“几乎”这个词承载了整段论证。珀涅罗珀可以体验亲密，却没有放弃所有权与命令权。她把危险工作交给女仆，要求她们引诱并监视求婚者，却没有把计划告诉欧律克勒娅。因此，女仆看似不忠的行为，部分是按照珀涅罗珀指令完成的角色表演，而这场表演的风险最终落在她们的身体上。

Atwood does not allow the language of sisterhood to erase class. Affection may be real, but it does not equalize the consequences of a decision. Penelope can describe the strategy; the maids are the ones accused of sexual betrayal and killed for it.

阿特伍德不允许“姐妹情谊”的语言抹去阶级。感情可以是真实的，却不会让一个决定造成的后果变得平等。珀涅罗珀可以讲述这项策略，而女仆才是因所谓性背叛遭到指控并被杀死的人。

## Sexual Violence and the Logic of Property
性暴力与财产逻辑

The maids describe childhoods shaped by poverty, sale, capture, and compulsory labour. As enslaved servants, they cannot meaningfully refuse the sexual demands of masters, guests, or socially powerful men. Yet after Odysseus returns, their contact with the suitors is treated as an offence against the household's male owner.

女仆们描述的童年由贫困、买卖、掳掠与强制劳动塑造。作为被奴役的仆人，她们无法真正拒绝主人、宾客或有权势男性的性要求。然而，奥德修斯归来之后，她们与求婚者的接触却被视为对家宅男性所有者的冒犯。

The contradiction is central: the social order does not protect the maids from rape, but it punishes them for being sexually used without their master's permission. Their bodies are treated less as selves capable of consent than as property whose authorized use belongs to someone else.

这里存在一个核心矛盾：社会秩序没有保护女仆免受强暴，却因为她们在未经男主人许可的情况下被性利用而惩罚她们。她们的身体较少被视为能够表达同意的主体，更多被视为一种财产，其合法使用权属于另一个人。

This is why the maids' story cannot be reduced to private jealousy or household disobedience. Their deaths expose a structure in which gender and class work together. Penelope is constrained as a woman, but she remains a queen. The maids are constrained as women, workers, enslaved people, and disposable members of the household.

因此，女仆的故事不能被简化为私人嫉妒或家庭不服从。她们的死亡揭示了一种性别与阶级共同运作的结构。珀涅罗珀作为女性受到限制，却仍然是一位王后；女仆则同时作为女性、劳动者、被奴役者与家中可以被牺牲的成员受到限制。

## The Hanging of the Twelve Maids
十二名女仆之死

After killing the suitors, Odysseus orders the maids to clean the blood from the hall. Telemachus then hangs them together. The method of death matters: the rope turns the girls into a terrible collective image, while the process gives them little opportunity to speak, defend themselves, or explain Penelope's instructions.

杀死求婚者之后，奥德修斯命令女仆清理大厅里的血迹，随后忒勒马科斯把她们一起绞死。死亡方式十分重要：绳索把女孩们变成一幅可怕的集体图像，同时让她们几乎没有机会说话、为自己辩护，或解释珀涅罗珀曾经给予的指令。

Penelope claims that she intended to tell Odysseus the truth but did not reach him in time. She also suspects that Eurycleia identified these particular maids out of resentment and a desire to control the household. These explanations distribute blame, but they do not clear Penelope. Her secrecy helped create the conditions in which the maids' actions could be read as betrayal.

珀涅罗珀声称自己原本打算告诉奥德修斯真相，却没能及时见到他。她也怀疑欧律克勒娅出于怨恨和控制家宅的愿望，特意指认了这些女仆。这些解释把责任分散到不同人物身上，却没有让珀涅罗珀获得完全清白。她的保密参与制造了一个条件，使女仆按照命令进行的行动可以被解释为背叛。

The maids' return as a chorus prevents their deaths from becoming a closed episode in a hero's homecoming. They haunt Penelope as much as Odysseus. The feminist demand for voice therefore becomes a demand for accountability: telling one's own story does not remove the need to answer the people harmed inside it.

女仆作为合唱队返回，使她们的死亡不再只是英雄返乡故事中已经结束的一幕。她们纠缠珀涅罗珀，也纠缠奥德修斯。因此，女性主义对声音的要求也变成了对责任的要求：讲述自己的故事，并不能取消回应故事中受害者的义务。

## The Anthropology Lecture
“人类学讲座”

In one of the book's boldest sections, the maids perform an academic lecture proposing that they were moon maidens in a matriarchal fertility cult and that their hanging records the destruction of an older religious order. The argument draws playfully on myth-and-ritual interpretations, including materials Atwood identifies in her acknowledgements.

在书中最大胆的段落之一，女仆们表演了一场学术讲座，提出自己曾是母系生育崇拜中的月亮少女，而她们遭到绞杀，记录了一个更古老宗教秩序的毁灭。这套论证戏仿神话—仪式学派的解释方式，也调用了阿特伍德在致谢中明确提及的材料。

The scene is not offered as a newly proven historical truth. Its exaggeration exposes the hunger to turn violated women into elegant symbols: lunar cycles, ritual numbers, fertility systems, and the defeat of matriarchy. Such theories may recover forgotten structures, but they can also move attention away from the plain fact that enslaved girls were killed.

这一场景并不是作为一项得到证明的新历史真相出现的。它的夸张揭露了人们把受害女性转化成优雅象征的冲动：月相循环、仪式数字、生育体系与母系社会的失败。这些理论或许能够找回被遗忘的结构，却也可能把注意力从一个朴素事实移开：被奴役的女孩遭到了杀害。

By letting the maids perform the theory themselves, Atwood gives them temporary control over scholarly language. They can become evidence, analysts, lecturers, and satirists at once. The section asks whether interpretation serves the dead or uses them again.

阿特伍德让女仆亲自表演这套理论，从而使她们暂时掌握了学术语言。她们同时成为证据、分析者、讲师与讽刺者。这个章节追问：解释究竟是在服务死者，还是再次使用她们？

## The Trial and the Problem of Historical Context
审判与“历史语境”的问题

The maids later stage a modern courtroom trial. Odysseus is defended for killing the suitors, and the maids demand that their own deaths be judged. The court acknowledges that they were raped and denied protection, yet the argument turns toward property: their sexual use occurred without the permission of the man who owned them.

女仆们后来表演了一场现代法庭审判。奥德修斯因杀死求婚者而接受辩护，女仆则要求法庭也审理她们的死亡。法庭承认她们遭到强暴且没有得到保护，论证却再次转向财产权：她们遭受性利用时，并未得到拥有她们的男人许可。

The judge hesitates to condemn practices that belonged to another historical period and dismisses the case. Athena then helps Odysseus escape. The parody targets a familiar failure of historical explanation: context can help us understand violence, but it becomes an alibi when understanding is treated as a reason not to judge or remember.

法官犹豫是否应该谴责属于另一个历史时期的行为，最后驳回案件；雅典娜随后帮助奥德修斯逃离。这里的戏仿指向一种熟悉的历史解释失败：语境能够帮助我们理解暴力，但如果“理解”被当作不再判断或记忆的理由，语境就会变成开脱。

The trial also shows that modern legal form does not automatically produce justice. The maids can finally enter the courtroom and speak, but the institution still decides that their claim is inconvenient, anachronistic, or insufficiently actionable. Voice without the power to compel an answer remains fragile.

这场审判也表明，现代法律形式不会自动产生正义。女仆终于能够进入法庭并发言，但制度仍然可以把她们的诉求判定为不便处理、不合时代或不足以采取行动。只有声音，却没有迫使对方回应的权力，这种发言仍然十分脆弱。

## A Feminist Retelling That Questions Its Own Heroine
一部质问自身女主人公的女性主义重写

*The Penelopiad* expands Penelope's agency without declaring her innocent. She is intelligent, constrained, politically vulnerable, and capable of preserving a household under pressure. She is also an owner of enslaved women whose dangerous labour protects her. Both descriptions must remain visible.

《珀涅罗珀记》扩展了珀涅罗珀的能动性，却没有宣布她无辜。她聪明、受到限制、处于政治危险之中，也有能力在压力下维持家宅；她同时拥有一群被奴役的女性，并依赖她们承担危险劳动来保护自己。这两种描述必须同时保持可见。

This complexity distinguishes the book from a retelling that merely discovers a hidden feminist icon. Patriarchal power does not place all women in the same position. A woman may resist the men above her while exercising authority over women below her. The maids' chorus keeps that vertical relation inside the feminist frame.

这种复杂性使本书区别于一种只想发现“被隐藏的女性主义偶像”的重写。父权权力并不会把所有女性放在同一位置。一个女人可以反抗位于她之上的男人，同时对位于她之下的女人行使权威。女仆合唱队迫使这条纵向关系始终留在女性主义画面之中。

The book's deepest revision may therefore be structural rather than biographical. It does not only ask what Penelope was really thinking. It creates a form in which no single narrator can secure the final meaning of the myth.

因此，这本书最深层的修订可能发生在结构上，而不只是人物传记层面。它不只追问珀涅罗珀当时究竟在想什么，还创造了一种没有任何单一叙述者能够垄断神话最终意义的形式。

## Why the Maids Keep Singing
为什么女仆仍在歌唱

The dead maids cannot be restored to life, given complete archives, or granted a belated legal victory. Their chorus offers something less conclusive and more persistent: interruption. Whenever Penelope's story begins to settle into coherence, they return to the rope, the work, the sexual violence, and the names that were never preserved.

死去的女仆无法复生，也无法获得完整档案或迟来的法律胜利。她们的合唱提供的是一种不那么圆满、却更持久的东西：打断。每当珀涅罗珀的故事开始形成连贯解释，她们就返回绳索、劳动、性暴力，以及那些从未被保存的名字。

Their final pursuit of Odysseus across different afterlives transforms haunting into historical memory. The hero would like another beginning; the maids refuse an ending without justice. They remain near the story because the story has not answered them.

她们最终穿过不同的来世追赶奥德修斯，把幽灵纠缠转化成历史记忆。英雄希望获得另一次开始，女仆却拒绝接受一个没有正义的结局。她们始终留在故事附近，因为这个故事还没有回答她们。

This is what the chorus opens for contemporary reading. Recovery cannot stop at finding the most famous woman excluded from a male-centred narrative. We must also ask which women remain beneath her restored voice, who performed the labour that made her survival possible, and whether our new version has learned to hear them.

这正是合唱队为今天的阅读打开的问题。找回女性不能停在发现男性中心叙事中那位最著名、却受到压抑的女人。我们还必须追问：在她被找回的声音之下，哪些女性仍然没有被听见？是谁承担了让她得以生存的劳动？我们的新版本是否真的学会倾听她们？

## Quotation｜引文

> “Now that all the others have run out of air, it's my turn to do a little story-making.”

> “既然其他人都已经耗尽了气息，现在轮到我来讲一点故事了。”

— Margaret Atwood, *The Penelopiad*, “A Low Art”

Penelope connects speech with survival. The dead have lost breath, but writing gives her another turn. Her phrase “story-making” is deliberately less authoritative than history: she admits that narration is made, arranged, and shaped by the teller.

珀涅罗珀把发言与生存连接起来。死者已经失去呼吸，写作却给了她另一次机会。她使用“制造故事”这个说法，有意不让自己的叙述看起来像不容置疑的历史：她承认叙事由讲述者制造、安排并赋形。

> “An edifying legend. A stick used to beat other women with.”

> “一个用于教化人的传奇。一根用来鞭打其他女性的棍子。”

— Margaret Atwood, *The Penelopiad*, “A Low Art”

The line turns praise into an instrument of discipline. Penelope's reputation does not remain attached only to her. Once made exemplary, it becomes a standard imposed on women who possess different desires, resources, and possibilities.

这句话把赞美转化成规训工具。珀涅罗珀的名声并不只附着于她本人；一旦成为典范，它便会变成一套施加给其他女性的标准，尽管那些女性拥有完全不同的欲望、资源与可能性。

> “we had no voice / we had no name / we had no choice”

> “我们没有声音 / 没有名字 / 没有选择。”

— Margaret Atwood, *The Penelopiad*, “The Chorus Line: We’re Walking Behind You, A Love Song”

The maids compress the inequalities of the book into three linked absences. Their lack of voice is connected to the loss of individual names and to the material impossibility of refusing work, sex, or punishment. The chorus gives them speech without pretending that belated representation can undo those conditions.

女仆把全书的不平等压缩成三个彼此相连的缺失：没有声音，与失去个人姓名及无法在物质上拒绝劳动、性支配或惩罚直接相关。合唱队给予她们发言，却没有假装迟来的再现能够取消那些条件。

## Scholarship｜学术文献

### Hilde Staels, “The Penelopiad and Weight: Contemporary Parodic and Burlesque Transformations of Classical Myths”

*College Literature*, vol. 36, no. 4, 2009, pp. 100–118. DOI: [10.1353/lit.0.0080](https://doi.org/10.1353/lit.0.0080).

Staels examines how Atwood and Jeanette Winterson use parody and burlesque to transform classical myth. Her account supports reading the maids' shifting genres not as decoration but as a formal challenge to the authority and elevated style of epic.

Staels 研究阿特伍德与珍妮特·温特森如何通过戏仿和滑稽化手法改造古典神话。她的论述支持我们把女仆不断变化的文体理解成对史诗权威及其崇高风格的形式挑战，而不只是装饰。

### Jasmine Richards, “Rereading Penelope's Web: The Anxieties of Female Authorship in Margaret Atwood's The Penelopiad”

In *Homer's Daughters: Women's Responses to Homer in the Twentieth Century and Beyond*, edited by Fiona Cox and Elena Theodorakopoulos, Oxford University Press, 2019, pp. 125–142. DOI: [10.1093/oso/9780198802587.003.0007](https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780198802587.003.0007).

Richards studies Penelope's weaving and the maids' chorus as representations of female authorship. The chapter is especially useful for understanding how Atwood draws on feminist interpretations of weaving while also confronting anxieties about women's authority, oral tradition, and the authenticity of their stories.

Richards 把珀涅罗珀的织布与女仆合唱队视为女性作者身份的文学再现。她的研究尤其有助于理解阿特伍德如何借用女性主义对织布的解释，同时面对围绕女性权威、口述传统与故事真实性产生的焦虑。

### Gerardo Rodríguez Salas, “‘Close as a Kiss’: The Challenge of the Maids' Gyn/Affection in Margaret Atwood's The Penelopiad”

*Amaltea. Revista de Mitocrítica*, vol. 7, 2015, pp. 19–34. DOI: [10.5209/rev.AMAL.2015.v7.47697](https://doi.org/10.5209/rev.AMAL.2015.v7.47697).

Rodríguez Salas argues that the murdered maids disrupt a reading centred only on Penelope's empowerment. His essay brings gender and class together and treats the maids' collective voice as an alternative form of female relation, writing, and myth-making.

Rodríguez Salas 指出，被杀女仆打断了一种只围绕珀涅罗珀获得赋权展开的阅读。他把性别与阶级放在一起，并把女仆的集体声音理解成女性关系、女性写作与神话创造的另一种形式。

### Mihoko Suzuki, “Rewriting the Odyssey in the Twenty-First Century: Mary Zimmerman's Odyssey and Margaret Atwood's Penelopiad”

*College Literature*, vol. 34, no. 2, 2007, pp. 263–278. DOI: [10.1353/lit.2007.0023](https://doi.org/10.1353/lit.2007.0023).

Suzuki compares two contemporary rewritings of *The Odyssey* and connects them to feminist reassessments of epic. Her discussion identifies the chorus, vaudeville, and burlesque as tools through which Atwood turns the silent maids into active satirists of the dominant order.

Suzuki 比较两部当代《奥德赛》重写，并把它们与女性主义对史诗的重新评估联系起来。她指出，合唱队、杂耍表演与滑稽戏是阿特伍德使用的重要工具，使沉默的女仆成为主导秩序的主动讽刺者。

### Margaret Atwood, “Introduction” and “Acknowledgements” to The Penelopiad

Atwood identifies the two questions that led to the book: what caused the hanging of the maids, and what Penelope was really doing. She also states that she has long been haunted by the maids. The acknowledgements name Homer's *Odyssey*, Robert Graves's mythographic accounts, Greek dramatic chorus, and satyr-play conventions among the materials she transformed.

阿特伍德在引言中明确提出促成这本书的两个问题：女仆为什么会被绞死，以及珀涅罗珀当时究竟在做什么。她也说明，那些被绞死的女仆长期困扰着她。致谢部分则列出荷马《奥德赛》、Robert Graves 的神话研究、希腊戏剧合唱队与萨堤尔剧传统等经她改造的材料。

## Related Works｜延伸阅读

### The Odyssey — Homer｜《奥德赛》— 荷马

Atwood's novella answers the epic's account of Penelope and the maids. Reading the two together makes visible how narrative focus, social rank, and translation determine who appears as a person and who appears only as a problem to be removed.

阿特伍德的小说直接回应史诗中对珀涅罗珀与女仆的处理。把两部作品放在一起阅读，可以看见叙事焦点、社会等级与翻译如何决定谁会作为一个人出现，谁只会作为需要清除的问题出现。

### Circe — Madeline Miller｜《喀耳刻》— 玛德琳·米勒

Miller retells another part of the Odyssean world through a woman previously organized around a male hero's journey. The novel can be compared with Atwood's sharper emphasis on collective voice, slavery, and unresolved guilt.

米勒通过一位过去主要围绕男性英雄旅程出现的女性，重写《奥德赛》世界的另一部分。它可以与阿特伍德对集体声音、奴役与未解决罪责的更尖锐关注进行对读。

### The Silence of the Girls — Pat Barker｜《女孩们的沉默》— 帕特·巴克

Barker's novel turns from celebrated warriors to captive women in the *Iliad*. Like *The Penelopiad*, it asks what heroic narrative looks like from the position of women treated as prizes, servants, and disposable bodies.

巴克的小说把视线从著名战士转向《伊利亚特》中的被俘女性。与《珀涅罗珀记》一样，它追问：当女性被当作战利品、仆役与可以牺牲的身体时，英雄叙事会呈现出什么样子？

### Lavinia — Ursula K. Le Guin｜《拉维尼娅》— 厄休拉·勒古恩

Le Guin gives interior life and narrative authority to the nearly silent woman from Virgil's *Aeneid*. Her retelling offers a useful comparison with Atwood's decision to divide authority between a named heroine and an anonymous chorus.

勒古恩为维吉尔《埃涅阿斯纪》中几乎沉默的女性赋予内在生活与叙事权。她的重写可以与阿特伍德把权威分配给一位有名女主人公和一群匿名合唱队的决定相互对照。
