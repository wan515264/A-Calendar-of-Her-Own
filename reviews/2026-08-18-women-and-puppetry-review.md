# Women and Puppetry

# 《女性与木偶》

## Who Is Allowed to Animate the World?

## 谁被允许赋予世界生命？

*Women and Puppetry: Critical and Historical Investigations* asks us to look beyond the figure onstage. A puppet may appear to be only cloth, wood, paper, or string, but its body is never culturally empty. Audiences learn to read gender into its face, costume, movement, voice, and story - and often treat an unmarked body as male by default.

《女性与木偶：批评与历史研究》要求我们把目光移到舞台形象之外。一个木偶看似只是布、木头、纸或线，但它的身体从来不是文化上的空白。观众会从面孔、服装、动作、声音与故事中读出性别，也常常默认没有明显性别标记的身体是男性。

The book also turns toward the people behind the figure. Across different historical and cultural settings, women have built puppets, repaired costumes, taught, written, organised, directed, and performed. Yet when puppetry carried sacred authority, professional status, or public influence, women were often barred from touching the puppet, entering the booth, or being remembered as artists.

这本书也把视线转向木偶背后的人。在不同历史与文化环境中，女性制作木偶、修补服装、教学、写作、组织、导演并参与表演。然而，当木偶戏拥有宗教权威、职业地位或公共影响力时，女性却常被禁止触碰木偶、进入戏棚，甚至无法作为艺术家被历史记住。

Women’s entry into puppetry therefore changes more than who holds the strings. It can alter which bodies become visible, which experiences receive a voice, and which traditions are allowed to change in order to remain alive.

因此，女性进入木偶艺术，改变的不只是由谁握住操纵线。它也会改变哪些身体可以被看见，哪些经验能够获得声音，以及一种传统是否可以通过改变而继续存活。

> “At historic moments when puppetry has held high value within a given culture, women are generally implicitly or explicitly excluded or marginalized.”
>
> “当木偶艺术在某种文化中拥有较高地位时，女性往往会受到隐性或显性的排斥与边缘化。”
>
> - Alissa Mello and Claudia Orenstein, “Introduction,” *Women and Puppetry*

## Critical and Historical Investigations

## 批评与历史研究

### A Field Seen from Behind the Curtain

### 从幕布背后重新观看一个领域

Published by Routledge in 2019, *Women and Puppetry: Critical and Historical Investigations* presents itself as the first book devoted specifically to women in puppetry arts. Its fifteen chapters bring together historical research, performance analysis, locally grounded case studies, and first-person reflections by practitioners. The volume is organised into three parts: critical perspectives, local contexts, and women practitioners speaking about their own work.

《女性与木偶：批评与历史研究》由 Routledge 于2019年出版，被出版社介绍为第一部专门研究木偶艺术中女性问题的著作。全书十五章结合历史研究、表演分析、扎根具体地区的案例，以及女性实践者的第一人称反思，并分为三个部分：批评视角、地方语境，以及女性实践者讲述自己的创作。

The editors begin from a double problem. Puppetry itself has often struggled for recognition within theatre and performance studies. At the same time, histories of the form have repeatedly centred celebrated male directors and the ideal of the solitary male genius. Women’s labour can therefore disappear twice: first because the art form is marginalised, and again because its own history marginalises women.

编者从一个双重问题出发。木偶艺术本身在戏剧与表演研究中长期难以获得充分承认；与此同时，这一领域的历史叙述又反复围绕著名男性导演与“孤独的男性天才”展开。因此，女性劳动可能遭遇两次消失：木偶艺术先被主流艺术史边缘化，女性又在木偶艺术自己的历史中被边缘化。

This absence does not prove that women were absent from practice. The book repeatedly asks us to distinguish a lack of surviving records from a lack of participation. Work carried out in homes, families, workshops, classrooms, rituals, and collaborative companies is especially easy to omit when history records only named directors, public performers, and formally recognised masters.

这种缺席并不能证明女性没有参与实践。全书反复提醒读者，必须区分“缺少留存记录”与“没有参与”这两件事。发生在家庭、作坊、教室、仪式与合作剧团中的劳动，尤其容易在历史只记录署名导演、公开表演者与正式大师时被遗漏。

### The Puppet Is Not a Neutral Body

### 木偶不是中性的身体

Laura Purcell-Gates’s chapter, “The Monster and the Corpse,” examines what happens when an apparently neutral puppet is presented as female. Her argument begins with a familiar convention: an unmarked puppet body is frequently read as male, while a female character is expected to acquire extra signs such as hair, lips, costume, gesture, or voice.

Laura Purcell-Gates 在《怪物与尸体》一章中讨论：当一个看似中性的木偶被呈现为女性时，会发生什么？她的论证从一种熟悉的惯例开始：没有标记的木偶身体常被理解为男性，而女性角色则被要求增加头发、嘴唇、服装、动作或声音等额外符号。

The supposed neutrality of the puppet is therefore not neutral. It conceals a hierarchy in which maleness functions as the unspoken norm and femininity appears as a marked variation. Yet puppetry can also expose this process. Because a puppet’s identity is visibly constructed through materials and performance, it can make the making of gender easier to notice.

因此，木偶所谓的中性并不真正中性。它遮蔽了一种等级：男性被当作无需说明的常态，女性则被表现成需要额外标记的变体。但木偶艺术也能够揭露这一过程。由于木偶身份明显通过材料与表演被制造出来，它反而让性别如何被制造变得更容易察觉。

This is one of the book’s most productive ideas. The puppet can repeat stereotypes with unusual clarity, but it can also disturb them. A body made of wood or cloth has no biological destiny. Every gendered cue has been selected, attached, voiced, and animated by someone.

这是全书最富启发性的观点之一。木偶可以格外清楚地复制刻板印象，也能够扰乱它们。木头或布制成的身体没有生理命运；它身上每一个性别线索，都是由某个人选择、添加、配音并赋予动作的。

### From “Woman as Puppet” to Woman as Puppeteer

### 从“女人如木偶”到女性成为操偶者

The editors trace a specifically Western linguistic history in which the puppet, doll, girl, and subordinate person became entangled. The English “puppet” developed from the Latin *pupa*, meaning a girl or doll, while “marionette” has been linked to a diminutive of Marie. By the early modern period, “puppet” could also be used as an insult for a person - especially a woman - imagined as gaudy, superficial, or easily controlled.

编者梳理了一个特定于西方语言史的脉络：木偶、玩偶、女孩与从属者逐渐纠缠在一起。英语 puppet 源于拉丁语 *pupa*，意为女孩或玩偶；marionette 则被认为与 Marie 的指小形式有关。到了近代早期，puppet 也可以成为对人的贬称，尤其用来形容被认为衣着浮夸、肤浅或容易受控制的女性。

The association creates a political reversal at the centre of this calendar entry. Woman has often been described as the puppet: decorative, speechless, and moved by another will. But women puppeteers occupy the position of maker, writer, director, manipulator, and animator. They do not merely appear as objects within a story; they participate in deciding how objects will move and what they will mean.

这一关联在本期日历中形成了一个政治性的反转。女性常被形容成木偶：具有装饰性、没有声音，并被另一种意志驱动。但女性操偶者占据的是制作者、作者、导演、操演者与赋予生命者的位置。她们不只作为故事中的物体出现，也参与决定物体如何行动，以及行动意味着什么。

This reversal should not be romanticised as complete control. Puppetry is collaborative, and the material object has weight, resistance, fragility, and its own range of possible movement. Still, the shift from being represented as manipulable to becoming a practitioner of manipulation makes questions of authorship and agency impossible to ignore.

这种反转不能被浪漫化成完全控制。木偶表演依赖合作，物质对象也拥有重量、阻力、脆弱性与自身可能的动作范围。尽管如此，从“被表现为可操控之物”转向“成为操演实践者”，仍然使作者身份与能动性的问题无法被忽视。

### Status, Exclusion, and the Paradox of Entry

### 地位、排斥与进入的悖论

One of the volume’s strongest historical observations concerns cultural status. In a number of traditions discussed by the contributors, puppetry was connected to sacred ritual, courtly power, or a viable profession. These forms could exclude women through rules about purity, public appearance, apprenticeship, touch, or access to ritual knowledge.

全书最有力量的历史观察之一，涉及艺术形式的文化地位。在书中讨论的若干传统里，木偶戏与宗教仪式、宫廷权力或可以维持生计的职业相连。这些传统可能通过关于纯洁、公开露面、师徒训练、触碰禁忌或仪式知识准入的规则排斥女性。

The book also identifies a troubling paradox. When a form loses prestige, income, or sacred authority, men may leave it, and women may find new opportunities to enter. Their access can therefore arrive not because equality has been achieved, but because the field has been reclassified as childish, domestic, educational, or culturally minor.

书中也指出一个令人不安的悖论：当一种艺术失去声望、收入或神圣权威时，男性可能离开，而女性反而获得进入机会。她们的准入并不一定意味着平等已经实现，也可能只是因为这个领域被重新归类为儿童化、家庭化、教育性或文化上次要的活动。

This pattern complicates any simple story of progress. Marginality can create room for experimentation and leadership, but it can also reproduce the assumption that work becomes suitable for women only after its authority has declined.

这种模式使任何简单的进步叙事都变得复杂。边缘地位可能为实验与领导创造空间，却也可能复制另一种假设：一项工作只有在权威下降之后，才变得“适合女性”。

### Whispering Women, Shouting Puppets

### 低声说话的女性，高声发言的木偶

Salma Mohseni Ardehali’s chapter on Iran carefully distinguishes public performance records from practices in women’s domestic and ritual spaces. Traditional public puppetry was historically dominated by men, while women developed forms of storytelling in all-female gatherings and sometimes animated dolls, household objects, and kitchen utensils. The archive is incomplete, so the chapter does not turn possibilities into certainty.

Salma Mohseni Ardehali 关于伊朗的章节，谨慎区分了公共表演记录与女性家庭、仪式空间中的实践。传统公共木偶戏长期由男性主导，女性则在全女性聚会中发展叙事表演，有时也让玩偶、家用物件与厨房器具参与表演。由于档案并不完整，这一章没有把可能性写成确定事实。

The establishment of academic puppetry training in Iran in the 1970s opened a different route into the field. The chapter describes how women later became active as directors, performers, designers, makers, writers, researchers, and teachers. Puppetry could offer a paradoxical shelter: a woman might remain physically concealed behind a puppet while using that puppet to address subjects difficult to stage through her visible body.

20世纪70年代伊朗学院木偶教育的建立，为女性进入这一领域开启了另一条路径。该章描述女性后来如何作为导演、表演者、设计者、制作者、作者、研究者与教师活跃起来。木偶艺术能够提供一种矛盾的掩护：女性的身体可以隐藏在木偶之后，同时又借助木偶讨论难以通过可见女性身体呈现的议题。

Concealment here is neither simple liberation nor simple silence. The hidden performer may gain a public voice, but the conditions that require her body to remain hidden still matter. The puppet becomes both screen and amplifier.

这里的隐藏既不是简单的解放，也不是简单的沉默。隐身的表演者可能获得公共声音，但迫使她隐藏身体的条件依然重要。木偶同时成为屏障与扩音器。

### When Judy Enters the Suffrage Fair

### 当 Judy 进入争取选举权的集市

Naomi Paxton’s chapter examines Punch and Judy performances associated with British suffrage fairs and exhibitions in the early twentieth century. The traditional show is built around anarchic comedy, but it also commonly includes Punch’s violence against Judy and gives female figures less agency than male or animal characters.

Naomi Paxton 的章节研究20世纪初英国争取女性选举权的集市与展览中出现的 Punch and Judy 表演。传统剧目建立在无政府式喜剧之上，却也常常包含 Punch 对 Judy 的暴力，并给予女性角色比男性或动物角色更少的行动空间。

The surviving archive does not preserve complete scripts for these suffrage performances. Paxton therefore uses limited evidence together with the documented performance culture of the suffrage movement to reconstruct what such adaptations may have done. This distinction is essential: the chapter offers a historically grounded argument, not a claim to possess a lost script.

现存档案没有保存这些女权表演的完整剧本。因此，Paxton 把有限证据与已有充分记录的选举权运动表演文化结合起来，重建这些改编可能如何运作。这个区别十分重要：该章提出的是有历史依据的论证，而不是声称自己掌握了一份已经失落的剧本。

Within suffrage spaces, a form known for silencing Judy could be redirected toward “votes for women,” domestic violence, sexual harassment, childcare, and women’s public speech. Reworking popular entertainment allowed activists to address audiences through a familiar form while exposing the gender politics already present inside it.

在选举权运动的空间里，一种以压制 Judy 声音闻名的表演，可以被重新引向“女性应有选举权”、家庭暴力、性骚扰、儿童照护与女性公共发言等议题。改写大众娱乐，使行动者能够借熟悉的形式接近观众，同时揭示这个形式内部原本就存在的性别政治。

### Women Do Not Enter One Universal Tradition

### 女性进入的并不是同一种普遍传统

The volume resists describing “women in puppetry” as a single universal experience. Access to performance in Myanmar, West Africa, Japan, Iran, Taiwan, Indonesia, Kenya, Europe, or the United States is shaped by distinct relations among religion, class, colonial history, family labour, educational institutions, state policy, and local ideas about gender.

全书拒绝把“木偶艺术中的女性”描述成一种普遍相同的经验。女性在缅甸、西非、日本、伊朗、台湾、印度尼西亚、肯尼亚、欧洲或美国进入表演领域的方式，都受到宗教、阶级、殖民历史、家庭劳动、教育制度、国家政策与地方性别观念之间不同关系的塑造。

This commitment to context prevents patriarchy from becoming an explanation that is too broad to explain anything. The recurring fact of gender inequality matters, but its mechanisms must be traced locally: who may touch sacred objects, who can travel, who receives training, who controls income, who speaks in public, and whose name enters the archive.

这种对具体语境的坚持，避免“父权制”变成一个过于宽泛、反而无法解释任何事情的答案。反复出现的性别不平等当然重要，但其机制必须在地方层面被追踪：谁可以触摸神圣物件，谁能够旅行，谁获得训练，谁控制收入，谁能公开发言，以及谁的名字能够进入档案。

### Making, Moving, and Being Remembered

### 制作、操演与被历史记住

The practitioners in the final section describe puppetry as more than the manipulation of a finished figure. Their work includes writing, making, directing, teaching, devising visual languages, organising companies, and negotiating how their own bodies relate to surrogate bodies onstage. Theodora Skipitares describes a thread running from her own female body to handmade objects and then to puppets used for large-scale stories.

最后一部分的实践者把木偶艺术描述为远不止操纵一个已经完成的形象。她们的工作包括写作、制作、导演、教学、发展视觉语言、组织剧团，以及协调自己的身体与舞台替身身体之间的关系。Theodora Skipitares 描述了一条创作线索：从她自己的女性身体延伸到手工物件，再延伸到用于讲述大型故事的木偶。

These accounts challenge the myth of the single genius. A performance may depend on makers, manipulators, costume workers, choreographers, writers, technicians, teachers, and producers, yet historical recognition can still gather around one name. Feminist history must therefore ask not only which women were absent, but whose visible authorship was built on labour that remained unnamed.

这些叙述挑战了“单一天才”的神话。一场表演可能依赖制作者、操偶者、服装人员、编舞、作者、技术人员、教师与制作人，但历史承认仍可能集中在一个名字上。因此，女性主义历史不仅要追问哪些女性缺席，也要追问哪些可见的作者身份建立在未被署名的劳动之上。

To be remembered as a puppeteer requires more than having done the work. It requires archives, criticism, institutions, teaching lineages, and histories willing to recognise collaborative and private labour as part of the art.

作为操偶艺术家被历史记住，并不只需要实际做过这项工作。它还需要档案、批评、制度、教学谱系，以及愿意把合作劳动与私人空间中的劳动承认为艺术组成部分的历史书写。

### Who Is Allowed to Animate the World?

### 谁被允许赋予世界生命？

Puppetry makes power unusually visible. One body appears to move because another body, sometimes hidden, supplies force, rhythm, and voice. But the relationship is never as simple as master and passive object. Materials resist; collaborators coordinate; traditions impose rules; audiences complete the illusion by agreeing to see life.

木偶艺术让权力变得格外可见。一个身体之所以看似能够行动，是因为另一个有时隐藏起来的身体为它提供力量、节奏与声音。但这种关系从来不只是主人与被动物体。材料会产生阻力，合作者必须协调，传统会施加规则，观众也需要同意看见生命，幻觉才得以完成。

A feminist approach therefore asks several questions at once. Who is represented as an object? Who is permitted to become an animator? Whose body must remain unseen? Who receives credit for collective work? And when a tradition changes hands, is it being lost - or is transformation the condition of its survival?

因此，女性主义视角会同时提出几个问题：谁被表现成物体？谁被允许成为赋予生命的人？谁的身体必须保持不可见？集体劳动最终归功于谁？当一种传统转入不同人的手中时，它是在消失，还是恰恰通过改变才得以延续？

The book’s answer is not that women simply replace men at the controls. Its more demanding proposal is that we reconsider the whole arrangement of bodies, objects, authority, labour, and memory through which a culture decides who can make the world move.

这本书的答案并不是让女性简单取代男性，站到操控位置上。它提出了一个更严格的要求：重新审视身体、物体、权威、劳动与记忆之间的整体安排，因为正是这套安排决定了一个社会允许谁让世界行动起来。

## Quotation｜引文

> “At historic moments when puppetry has held high value within a given culture, women are generally implicitly or explicitly excluded or marginalized.”
>
> “当木偶艺术在某种文化中拥有较高地位时，女性往往会受到隐性或显性的排斥与边缘化。”
>
> - Alissa Mello and Claudia Orenstein, “Introduction,” *Women and Puppetry: Critical and Historical Investigations*, p. 5

This sentence identifies a pattern without pretending that every tradition is the same. Exclusion may take the form of ritual prohibition, restricted apprenticeship, professional gatekeeping, limits on women’s public presence, or the historical failure to record their labour.

这句话指出了一种反复出现的模式，却没有假装所有传统完全相同。排斥可能表现为仪式禁忌、师徒制度限制、职业把关、对女性公开露面的约束，或者历史记录未能保存她们的劳动。

It also reveals why visibility alone is not enough. A female puppet can be highly visible while the woman who made, voiced, costumed, repaired, or manipulated it remains unknown. Feminist criticism must move between the body onstage and the structures behind it.

它也揭示了为什么仅仅“看得见女性形象”并不够。一个女性木偶可以非常醒目，而制作、配音、装扮、修复或操演它的女性仍然无人知晓。女性主义批评必须在舞台上的身体与它背后的结构之间来回移动。

Finally, the sentence makes cultural value itself part of the analysis. Prestige is not neutral. When an art gains sacred, professional, or economic authority, control over participation can become more severe. When it loses status, new doors may open - but often on unequal terms.

最后，这句话也把文化价值本身纳入分析。声望从来不是中性的。当一种艺术获得神圣、职业或经济权威时，对参与资格的控制可能变得更加严格；当它失去地位时，新的入口可能出现，但这些入口往往建立在不平等条件之上。
