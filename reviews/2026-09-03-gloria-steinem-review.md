**Writer / Journalist / Feminist Organizer｜作家 / 记者 / 女性主义组织者**

## A Movement Is More Than Its Most Famous Face

## 一场运动大于它最著名的面孔

## Today Card｜首页卡片版

Gloria Steinem became one of the most recognizable public figures of the modern women's movement. She reported on gendered working conditions, spoke publicly for abortion rights, helped build *Ms.* magazine, travelled with organizers, raised funds, supported political candidates, and co-founded institutions intended to outlast any single campaign. When she died in New York City on September 2, 2026, at the age of ninety-two, many accounts described her as the face or symbol of American feminism.

格洛丽亚·斯泰纳姆成为现代女性运动中最具辨识度的公共人物之一。她报道受到性别塑造的劳动条件，公开争取堕胎权，参与创办 *Ms.* 杂志，与组织者共同巡回演讲，为运动筹款，支持政治候选人，也参与建立能够超越单次行动而持续存在的机构。2026年9月2日，她在纽约家中去世，享年92岁。许多报道再次把她称为美国女性主义的面孔或象征。

That visibility created political access, but it also created a problem. A diverse movement involving Black feminists, working-class organizers, lesbians, welfare-rights activists, lawyers, writers, and local groups could be compressed by the media into the image of one famous white woman. Steinem used celebrity to carry feminist arguments into mainstream journalism, yet no celebrity can adequately represent a collective struggle.

这种可见性带来了政治入口，也制造了一个问题。一场由黑人女性主义者、工人阶级组织者、女同性恋者、福利权利行动者、律师、作家与地方团体共同组成的多元运动，可能被媒体压缩成一位著名白人女性的形象。斯泰纳姆利用名人身份把女性主义论述带进主流新闻，但任何名人都不足以代表一场集体斗争。

Her most durable lesson may therefore lie not in the idea of a perfect leader, but in the relationship between voice and structure. An article can give an experience a name. A magazine can let readers discover that they are not alone. A speaking tour can connect local groups. An organization can turn recognition into continuing work. The movement becomes real when its audience becomes participants.

因此，她最持久的启示或许不在于塑造一位完美领袖，而在于声音与结构之间的关系。一篇文章可以为一种经验命名；一本杂志可以让读者发现自己并不孤单；一次巡回演讲可以连接地方团体；一个组织可以把认同转化为持续行动。当听众成为参与者，运动才真正开始移动。

> “A movement is a contagion of truth telling: at last, we know we are not alone.”
>
> “一场运动是真相讲述的传播：我们终于知道，自己并不孤单。”

— Gloria Steinem, foreword to *50 Years of Ms.*, 2023

## Detail Page｜详细介绍页

### Gloria Steinem｜格洛丽亚·斯泰纳姆

### 1934–2026

### Writer / Journalist / Feminist Organizer｜作家 / 记者 / 女性主义组织者

Gloria Marie Steinem was born in Toledo, Ohio, in 1934. Her early childhood involved frequent travel with her father, an itinerant antiques dealer. After her parents separated, she lived with and helped care for her mother, Ruth, who had worked as a journalist before marriage and experienced serious mental illness. Steinem later connected her mother's restricted life with the social conditions that denied many women economic independence, professional recognition, and adequate care.

Gloria Marie Steinem 于1934年出生在俄亥俄州托莱多。她童年早期经常跟随从事流动古董生意的父亲旅行。父母分开后，她与母亲 Ruth 共同生活并承担照护责任。Ruth 婚前曾做过记者，后来长期受到严重精神疾病困扰。斯泰纳姆日后把母亲受到限制的人生，与许多女性缺少经济独立、职业承认与充分照护的社会条件联系起来。

She graduated from Smith College in 1956 and then spent approximately two years in India. After returning to the United States, she worked for the Independent Research Service, which sent American participants to Soviet-sponsored international youth festivals. The organization received covert CIA funding, a fact disclosed publicly in 1967. Steinem acknowledged and defended her role at the time. This episode remains a documented and contested part of her political history; its existence should neither be concealed nor treated, without further evidence, as proof that all of her later feminist work was directed by an intelligence agency.

她于1956年毕业于史密斯学院，随后在印度生活约两年。回到美国后，她曾为 Independent Research Service 工作，该组织安排美国参与者前往由苏联支持的国际青年节，并接受中央情报局秘密资助。这一事实于1967年被公开。斯泰纳姆当时承认并为自己的角色辩护。这段经历是她政治生涯中有文献依据且持续存在争议的一部分；它不应被隐瞒，也不能在缺乏进一步证据的情况下，被直接当作她此后全部女性主义工作都受到情报机构操控的证明。

## Journalism Before Celebrity｜成名之前的新闻工作

Steinem entered a journalism industry in which editors routinely assigned women to fashion, food, service writing, and research while reserving political reporting and authoritative commentary for men. In 1963 she worked undercover for about a month as a server at New York's Playboy Club. Her two-part article, “A Bunny's Tale,” documented low pay, bodily surveillance, sexualized rules, and the gap between Playboy's glamorous image and the working conditions endured by its employees.

斯泰纳姆进入新闻行业时，编辑通常把时尚、饮食、服务性写作与资料研究分配给女性，却把政治报道与权威评论留给男性。1963年，她在纽约 Playboy Club 以服务员身份卧底工作约一个月。她发表的两篇“A Bunny's Tale”记录了低薪、身体监控、性化规章，以及 Playboy 的魅力形象与雇员实际劳动条件之间的落差。

The investigation brought public attention but did not immediately establish her as a serious political journalist. Steinem later said that the assignment made it harder to obtain other work because editors continued to identify her with the costume she had worn to expose the club. The episode reveals a recurring problem in her career: appearance could provide access to media while also being used to diminish the labour behind her reporting.

这次调查带来了公众关注，却没有立即让她被承认为严肃的政治记者。斯泰纳姆后来表示，这项任务反而使她更难获得其他工作，因为编辑持续把她与为了揭露俱乐部而穿上的服装联系起来。这段经历揭示了她职业生涯中反复出现的问题：外表可以为她打开媒体入口，也可以被用来贬低报道背后的劳动。

## The Abortion Speak-Out Changed the Direction of Her Work｜堕胎权公开证言改变了她的工作方向

In 1969 Steinem attended a New York speak-out at which women testified about abortion. She had undergone an illegal abortion in London at the age of twenty-two but had kept the experience private. Hearing other women speak publicly helped her recognize that an experience treated as an individual secret was structured by law, medicine, money, and gendered power. She later described the event as the beginning of her activism in the women's movement.

1969年，斯泰纳姆参加了纽约一场由女性公开讲述堕胎经历的活动。她22岁时曾在伦敦接受当时属于非法行为的堕胎手术，却长期对此保持沉默。听见其他女性公开发言，使她认识到，一种被处理成个人秘密的经验，实际上受到法律、医疗、金钱与性别权力共同塑造。她后来把这次活动称为自己参与女性运动的起点。

The importance of the speak-out was methodological as well as political. Testimony converted isolation into evidence. When many people described related experiences, what had appeared to be private misfortune could be understood as a public structure. Steinem carried this method into journalism and organizing: listen, connect recurring experiences, give them language, and create a form through which people can act together.

这场公开证言的重要性既是政治性的，也是方法性的。证言把孤立经验转化成证据。当许多人描述相互关联的经历时，原本看似私人的不幸便能够被理解为一种公共结构。斯泰纳姆把这种方法带入新闻与组织工作：倾听，把反复出现的经验连接起来，为它们提供语言，再创造一种让人们能够共同采取行动的形式。

## Ms. Was an Institution, Not One Woman's Magazine｜Ms. 是一个机构，而不是一个女人的杂志

*Ms.* first appeared as a sample insert in *New York* magazine in December 1971, followed by a stand-alone preview issue in 1972. Its founding and survival were collective achievements involving editors, writers, organizers, fundraisers, readers, and staff including Patricia Carbine, Suzanne Braun Levine, Joanne Edgar, Nina Finkelstein, Mary Thom, Letty Cottin Pogrebin, and many others. To call it simply “Gloria Steinem's magazine” reproduces the same celebrity logic that obscures collective labour.

*Ms.* 于1971年12月首先以 *New York* 杂志试刊插页的形式出现，随后在1972年推出独立预览刊。它的创办与延续是一项集体成就，参与者包括编辑、作家、组织者、筹款者、读者与工作人员，其中有 Patricia Carbine、Suzanne Braun Levine、Joanne Edgar、Nina Finkelstein、Mary Thom、Letty Cottin Pogrebin 以及许多其他女性。把它简单称作“Gloria Steinem 的杂志”，会重复那套遮蔽集体劳动的名人逻辑。

The magazine treated subjects commonly separated into different editorial departments as parts of one political world: employment, childcare, welfare, abortion, sexuality, language, electoral politics, racism, literature, health, and violence. Its first issue included welfare-rights organizer Johnnie Tillmon's “Welfare Is a Women's Issue,” making clear that paid and unpaid labour, poverty, race, and gender belonged within feminist debate.

这本杂志把通常被分配到不同编辑部门的主题，视作同一个政治世界的组成部分：就业、儿童照护、福利、堕胎、性、语言、选举政治、种族主义、文学、健康与暴力。创刊号刊登了福利权利组织者 Johnnie Tillmon 的“Welfare Is a Women's Issue”，明确表明有偿与无偿劳动、贫困、种族与性别都属于女性主义讨论。

*Ms.* also confronted the economic contradiction of publishing feminist journalism in an advertising market. Steinem's 1990 essay “Sex, Lies and Advertising” documented pressure from advertisers who expected women's magazines to maintain an editorial environment flattering to their products. The struggle was not only to let women write, but to build material conditions in which writing controlled by women could remain independent.

*Ms.* 也必须面对一个经济矛盾：如何在广告市场中出版女性主义新闻。斯泰纳姆1990年的文章“Sex, Lies and Advertising”记录了广告商施加的压力。广告商期待女性杂志提供有利于其产品的编辑环境。因此，斗争不只是让女性获得写作机会，也在于建立物质条件，使由女性掌握的写作能够保持独立。

## Celebrity as a Political Tool and a Distortion｜名人身份既是政治工具，也是扭曲机制

Television and print journalism repeatedly presented Steinem as the movement's most acceptable, glamorous, or photogenic representative. This visibility enabled her to carry feminist arguments into spaces that dismissed more radical organizers. It also directed attention, invitations, money, and historical memory toward her. Celebrity did not simply reflect leadership; it helped manufacture a hierarchy of whose feminism became publicly legible.

电视与纸媒不断把斯泰纳姆呈现成女性运动中最容易被接受、最有魅力或最适合上镜的代表。这种可见性使她能够把女性主义论述带入那些排斥更激进组织者的空间，也让关注、邀请、资金与历史记忆不断流向她。名人身份并不只是反映领导地位，它也参与制造一种等级，决定谁的女性主义能够被公众理解。

Steinem did not create this media structure alone, and she sometimes used her access to redirect attention. Her early speaking partnership with Black feminist and child-welfare organizer Dorothy Pitman Hughes helped connect public talks with questions of race, family, housing, and community resources. She also travelled and worked with lawyer and activist Florynce Kennedy. Yet remembering these collaborations only as supporting chapters in Steinem's biography would again place the famous white figure at the centre. Hughes, Kennedy, Shirley Chisholm, Fannie Lou Hamer, Johnnie Tillmon, Dolores Huerta, Wilma Mankiller, and many others possessed political projects and histories of their own.

斯泰纳姆并非独自创造这套媒体结构，她有时也利用自己的入口转移公众注意。她早期与黑人女性主义者、儿童福利组织者 Dorothy Pitman Hughes 搭档巡回演讲，把公共讨论与种族、家庭、住房及社区资源联系起来。她也曾与律师和行动者 Florynce Kennedy 一起旅行和工作。然而，如果我们只把这些合作记成斯泰纳姆传记中的辅助章节，仍会把著名白人女性放在中心。Hughes、Kennedy、Shirley Chisholm、Fannie Lou Hamer、Johnnie Tillmon、Dolores Huerta、Wilma Mankiller 以及许多其他行动者，都拥有各自的政治计划与历史。

## Satire Reveals How Power Invents Logic｜讽刺揭示权力如何发明逻辑

Steinem's 1978 essay “If Men Could Menstruate” remains one of her clearest demonstrations of feminist satire. It imagines menstruation as a male bodily function and predicts that institutions would then redefine it as proof of strength, authority, religious fitness, and political competence. The joke exposes a mechanism of domination: traits possessed by powerful groups are praised, while traits associated with subordinated groups are used to justify their exclusion.

斯泰纳姆1978年的文章“If Men Could Menstruate”仍是她运用女性主义讽刺最清楚的例子之一。文章设想月经是一种男性身体功能，并推测各种制度将会把它重新定义成力量、权威、宗教资格与政治能力的证明。这个笑话揭露了一种支配机制：强势群体拥有的特征会被赞美，而与弱势群体联系在一起的特征则被用来为排斥辩护。

The essay's force comes from changing the social position while leaving the bodily process recognizable. Biology does not explain its own meaning. Institutions decide whether blood signifies pollution or courage, whether pain deserves research, and whether a body is treated as an obstacle to public authority. The reversal does not prove that every gendered experience is identical; it asks who possesses the power to turn difference into hierarchy.

这篇文章的力量来自改变社会位置，同时让身体过程保持可辨认。生理不会自动解释自身意义。是制度决定血液意味着污秽还是勇气，疼痛是否值得研究，以及某种身体是否被当作获得公共权威的障碍。这种反转并不是证明所有性别经验都完全相同，而是追问谁拥有把差异转化成等级的权力。

## Organizing Means Building Relationships｜组织工作意味着建立关系

Steinem's public image was attached to podiums and television studios, but much of her work occurred through travel, fundraising, meetings, introductions, correspondence, and repeated support for organizations. She helped establish or sustain the National Women's Political Caucus, Women's Action Alliance, Ms. Foundation for Women, Voters for Choice, Equality Now, and Women's Media Center, among other initiatives.

斯泰纳姆的公共形象常与讲台和电视演播室联系在一起，但她的大量工作发生在旅行、筹款、会议、引荐、通信以及对组织的持续支持之中。她参与创办或维持的机构包括 National Women's Political Caucus、Women's Action Alliance、Ms. Foundation for Women、Voters for Choice、Equality Now 与 Women's Media Center 等。

In *My Life on the Road*, she emphasizes listening in rooms where people can speak from experience and recognize one another. This model places political knowledge in conversation rather than only in expert declaration. It also explains why a magazine mattered to her as more than a delivery system for finished opinions: letters from readers could reveal patterns, correct assumptions, and turn a publication into an organizing network.

在 *My Life on the Road* 中，她强调在同一个房间里倾听，让人们能够从经验出发发言并相互辨认。这种模式把政治知识放进对话，而不只放进专家宣言。它也解释了为什么杂志对她而言不只是传递完成观点的工具：读者来信可以揭示反复出现的模式、纠正假设，并把出版物转化成组织网络。

## A Legacy Must Include Disagreement｜遗产必须容纳分歧

Steinem's prominence made her a frequent target of anti-feminist hostility, but criticism also came from within feminism. Radical and socialist feminists challenged the reformist orientation of mainstream institutions, the influence of corporate media, and Steinem's early CIA-funded work. Black feminists and other women of color disputed public narratives that treated white middle-class women as the origin or universal subject of feminism. Later scholars have examined both Steinem's interracial alliances and moments when her writing risked treating women as a unified category.

斯泰纳姆的知名度使她频繁成为反女性主义攻击的对象，但批评也来自女性主义内部。激进女性主义者与社会主义女性主义者质疑主流机构的改良主义方向、企业媒体的影响，以及她早年参与接受 CIA 资助的项目。黑人女性主义者与其他有色人种女性反对把白人中产阶级女性写成女性主义起源或普遍主体的公共叙事。后来的研究者既考察斯泰纳姆跨种族合作的实践，也分析她的写作何时可能把女性处理成一个过于统一的类别。

These disagreements should not be flattened into a verdict that she was either the inventor of modern feminism or an illegitimate media creation. Both accounts mistake a movement for a biography. A historically responsible assessment asks what her work made possible, whose labour made her work possible, what her institutions included or left unresolved, and how unequal visibility shaped the record inherited by later generations.

这些分歧不应被压缩成非此即彼的判决：她既不是现代女性主义的单独发明者，也不能被简单视作毫无正当性的媒体产物。这两种说法都把一场运动误认成一个人的传记。更负责任的历史评估应当追问：她的工作打开了什么可能，谁的劳动使她的工作成为可能，她参与建立的机构容纳了什么又留下了什么问题，以及不平等的可见性如何塑造后代继承的历史记录。

## After the Famous Face｜在著名面孔之后

Steinem's death arrived during another period of intense conflict over reproductive freedom, voting rights, media power, and the meaning of feminism. Her life cannot resolve those struggles. It can show how political language travels: from a private experience to a speak-out, from a local conversation to a national magazine, from a public figure to an institution, and from one generation's unfinished work to the next.

斯泰纳姆去世时，围绕生育自由、投票权、媒体权力与女性主义意义的冲突再次十分激烈。她的一生无法替我们解决这些斗争，却能够显示政治语言如何移动：从私人经验进入公开证言，从地方对话进入全国杂志，从公共人物进入制度，再从一代人未完成的工作进入下一代。

To remember her accurately is therefore to resist two forms of erasure. One would erase her journalism, organizing, fundraising, humour, and persistence because she was imperfect. The other would erase the many people around her by allowing one recognizable face to stand for the movement. A movement survives when memory becomes plural and when admiration does not end inquiry.

因此，准确记住她意味着拒绝两种遗忘。一种遗忘因为她并不完美，就抹去她的新闻工作、组织、筹款、幽默与坚持；另一种遗忘则让一张容易辨认的面孔代表整场运动，从而抹去她周围的许多人。一场运动只有在记忆保持复数、敬意没有终止追问时，才能继续存在。

## Quotation｜引文

> “A movement is a contagion of truth telling: at last, we know we are not alone.”
>
> “一场运动是真相讲述的传播：我们终于知道，自己并不孤单。”

— Gloria Steinem, foreword to *50 Years of Ms.: The Best of the Pathfinding Magazine That Ignited a Revolution*, 2023

The sentence describes collective recognition rather than personal conversion. A person may already know that an experience is painful or unjust, yet still understand it as an isolated failure. Truth telling becomes political when one account encounters another and reveals a pattern.

这句话描述的不是个人思想的突然转变，而是集体性的相互辨认。一个人可能已经知道某种经历令人痛苦或并不公正，却仍把它理解成孤立的个人失败。当一种讲述遇见另一种讲述，并共同揭示出某种结构，真相讲述才成为政治行动。

Calling a movement a “contagion” also shifts attention away from a central authority. The idea spreads horizontally through testimony, reading, listening, imitation, argument, and action. No famous spokesperson owns the process, even when her words help it travel.

把运动称为一种“传播”，也使注意力离开单一中心权威。观念通过证言、阅读、倾听、模仿、争论与行动横向扩散。即使一位著名发言者的话语帮助它移动，她也不拥有这个过程。

The final phrase, “we are not alone,” does not promise agreement on every question. It names the minimum condition for collective politics: recognition that private isolation has social causes and that other people can become collaborators rather than distant examples.

最后一句“我们并不孤单”并不承诺所有人会在每个问题上达成一致。它命名的是集体政治的最低条件：认识到私人孤立具有社会原因，也认识到其他人可以成为共同工作者，而不只是遥远的例子。
