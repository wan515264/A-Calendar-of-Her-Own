## Cléo from 5 to 7｜《五至七时的克莱奥》

Film / French New Wave / Feminist Cinema / Time｜电影 / 法国新浪潮 / 女性主义电影 / 时间

## From Being Looked At to Looking at the World
从被观看，到观看世界

## Today Card｜首页卡片版

Cléo is a young singer waiting for the results of a medical examination. For ninety minutes, she moves through Paris among mirrors, hats, cafés, songs, taxis, strangers, friends, and street noise. At first, she tries to manage fear by confirming that she is still beautiful. If her image remains intact, perhaps death can be kept at a distance.

克莱奥是一位等待医学检查结果的年轻歌手。在九十分钟里，她穿过巴黎的镜子、帽子店、咖啡馆、歌曲、出租车、陌生人、朋友与街道噪音。最初，她试图通过确认自己仍然美丽来控制恐惧：只要镜中的形象没有破裂，死亡似乎就还能被推远。

But Agnès Varda gradually changes the direction of vision. Cléo removes the blonde wig that helps compose her public image, leaves the people who infantilise her, and walks alone. The city is no longer only a stage on which she is seen. She begins to notice faces, bodies, labour, danger, illness, war, and lives continuing beside her own.

但阿涅斯·瓦尔达逐渐改变了观看的方向。克莱奥摘下构成她公众形象的金色假发，离开那些把她当作孩子的人，独自走上街头。城市不再只是供她被观看的舞台。她开始注意面孔、身体、劳动、危险、疾病、战争，以及与她的生命并行发生的其他人生。

The film does not present this change as a complete escape from fear or from other people's gaze. Cléo is still frightened, still visible, and still vulnerable. What changes is her relation to the world: she becomes capable of looking outward and of meeting another person without performing the role of “Cléo” quite so completely.

影片没有把这种变化写成对恐惧或他人目光的彻底逃离。克莱奥依然害怕，依然处于可见状态，也依然脆弱。真正改变的是她与世界的关系：她开始能够向外观看，也开始能够在不完全表演“克莱奥”这个角色的情况下与另一个人相遇。

> “Today everything amazes me. People's faces next to mine.”

> “今天，一切都让我惊讶。别人的脸，就在我的脸旁边。”

— Cléo, *Cléo from 5 to 7*

## Ninety Minutes of Waiting
九十分钟的等待

Written and directed by Agnès Varda and released in 1962, *Cléo from 5 to 7* follows a singer, played by Corinne Marchand, as she waits to learn the results of a biopsy. Although the title promises two hours, the film lasts approximately eighty-nine minutes and follows Cléo from about five o'clock until half past six. Varda described the formal challenge as creating a story in real time and real geography, “one minute after another,” without cheating the route through Paris in the edit. [Criterion](https://www.criterion.com/films/244) lists the film at eighty-nine minutes; Varda discusses the real-time and geographical design in a [2017 conversation](https://brooklynrail.org/2017/04/art/agnes-varda/).

《五至七时的克莱奥》由阿涅斯·瓦尔达编剧并执导，于1962年上映。影片跟随科琳娜·马尔尚饰演的歌手克莱奥，记录她等待活检结果的过程。虽然片名承诺了两个小时，影片实际长度约八十九分钟，故事从下午五点左右持续到六点半左右。瓦尔达曾把这项形式挑战描述为：在真实时间与真实地理中讲述故事，让一分钟接着一分钟发生，不通过剪辑伪造克莱奥穿越巴黎的路线。[Criterion](https://www.criterion.com/films/244) 将影片长度列为八十九分钟；瓦尔达也在一次[2017年的访谈](https://brooklynrail.org/2017/04/art/agnes-varda/)中谈到实时与地理设计。

Waiting is not empty time here. Every minute is charged by the possibility of cancer. Ordinary actions—buying a hat, drinking coffee, rehearsing a song, taking a taxi, visiting a friend, riding a bus—become ways of enduring uncertainty. Clocks and chapter headings repeatedly place Cléo inside measurable public time, while fear stretches and contracts her private experience of the same minutes.

等待在这里并不是空白时间。癌症的可能性使每一分钟都带有压力。买帽子、喝咖啡、排练歌曲、乘出租车、拜访朋友、坐公交车，这些普通行动都变成了承受不确定性的方式。时钟与章节标题不断把克莱奥放进可以测量的公共时间，而恐惧则让她对同一分钟的私人体验不断伸长或收缩。

## Tarot, Colour, and the Fear of an Ending
塔罗牌、色彩与结局的恐惧

The film opens with a tarot reading shown in colour. Hands turn over cards while Cléo and the fortune-teller interpret images of illness, transformation, and death. When the camera finally reveals faces, the film changes to black and white. This opening gives colour to a symbolic system before returning Cléo to the physical city where no image can provide certainty.

影片以一场彩色的塔罗牌占卜开始。手掌翻开一张张牌，克莱奥与占卜师从中解读疾病、转变与死亡的图像。当摄影机最终显露人物面孔时，画面转为黑白。这个开场先把色彩交给一套象征系统，随后才让克莱奥回到具体的城市；在那里，没有任何图像能够提供确定答案。

Cléo is superstitious, but the film does not treat superstition simply as foolishness. She has received little clear medical information and has been left to wait. In that gap, signs multiply: cards, broken mirrors, funeral processions, songs, street accidents, and bodily sensations all seem capable of predicting the future. The hunger for omens emerges from a situation in which knowledge about her own body is controlled elsewhere.

克莱奥迷信，但影片并没有把迷信简单处理成愚蠢。她没有获得清晰的医学信息，只能被留下等待。在这个信息缺口中，征兆不断增殖：塔罗牌、破碎的镜子、送葬队伍、歌曲、街头事故与身体感觉，似乎都可能预言未来。她对征兆的渴求，来自一种关于自身身体的知识被掌握在别处的处境。

## Beauty as Protection
把美貌当作保护

After leaving the fortune-teller, Cléo looks at herself in a mirror and reassures herself through beauty. Her reflection offers a defence: a beautiful face appears whole, alive, and untouched by whatever may be happening inside the body. Her early statement, often translated as “As long as I'm beautiful, I'm alive,” condenses the bargain she has learned to make with visibility.

离开占卜师之后，克莱奥看向镜子，用美貌安慰自己。倒影提供了一种防御：一张漂亮的脸看起来完整、鲜活，似乎没有受到体内变化的影响。她在影片前段说出的那句话，常被译作“只要我仍然美丽，我就活着”，它浓缩了她与可见性之间习得的交换关系。

Beauty gives Cléo real social advantages. Shop assistants flatter her, strangers notice her, and the people around her organise themselves around her moods. Yet beauty also narrows what others are willing to recognise. Her fear is dismissed as caprice, her sadness is treated as theatrical, and her musical collaborators package her voice inside a marketable feminine persona.

美貌确实给予克莱奥社会优势。店员恭维她，陌生人注意她，身边的人也围绕她的情绪行动。但美貌同时缩小了他人愿意承认的东西：她的恐惧被当作任性，她的悲伤被视为表演，而音乐合作者则把她的声音包装进一种可以出售的女性形象。

## Mirrors Do Not Simply Disappear
镜子并没有简单消失

Mirrors recur throughout the film, but their meaning changes. At first, Cléo uses them to verify herself through appearance. She looks in order to see the image others are expected to desire. Later, reflections become fragmented, displaced, or less reassuring. The film does not announce that one authentic self has been hiding behind a false surface. Instead, it shows how a self can be composed through repeated acts of looking and being looked at.

镜子在影片中反复出现，但意义不断改变。最初，克莱奥通过外貌在镜中确认自己；她观看，是为了看见他人理应渴望的形象。后来，倒影变得破碎、偏移，或者不再能够提供安慰。影片并没有宣布某个“真实自我”一直藏在虚假表面之后，而是展示一个自我如何在反复观看与被观看的过程中被构成。

This is why it is too simple to say that Cléo moves cleanly from object to subject. She does gain a more active relation to vision, but she never leaves the social field in which bodies are read, judged, desired, and classified. Jill Forbes's scholarship is useful here because it complicates celebratory accounts of empowerment and asks how Cléo remains mapped onto gendered traditions of imagining Paris and women. [“Gender and Space in Cléo de 5 à 7”](https://doi.org/10.1386/sfci.2.2.83)

因此，把克莱奥的变化概括成从“客体”干净利落地转向“主体”，仍然过于简单。她确实获得了更主动的观看关系，却从未离开那个对身体进行阅读、判断、欲望化与分类的社会场域。Jill Forbes 的研究在这里尤其重要，因为它使单纯庆祝“赋权”的解释变得复杂，并追问克莱奥如何仍被放置在关于巴黎与女性的性别化传统中。[《〈五至七时的克莱奥〉中的性别与空间》](https://doi.org/10.1386/sfci.2.2.83)

## “Everybody Spoils Me; Nobody Loves Me”
“每个人都宠我，却没有人爱我”

Cléo's apartment appears luxurious, but the attention she receives there is strangely impersonal. Her lover arrives briefly and speaks to a version of her that is charming, delicate, and available for affection. Angèle manages her schedule and protects her from information. The musicians tease, flatter, and direct her. Cléo is surrounded, yet rarely addressed as someone capable of confronting the truth.

克莱奥的公寓看起来十分奢华，但她在那里获得的关注却奇异地缺少个人性。情人短暂到访，与一个迷人、纤弱、适合接受宠爱的克莱奥交谈；安热勒替她管理日程，也试图把某些信息挡在她之外；音乐人则打趣、恭维并指导她。克莱奥被人群包围，却很少被当作一个有能力面对真相的人。

Her complaint that everybody spoils her but nobody loves her distinguishes care from indulgence. To spoil someone can preserve dependence: gifts and reassurance substitute for listening, honesty, and recognition. The line also reveals why apparent privilege does not cancel loneliness.

她抱怨“每个人都宠我，却没有人爱我”，由此区分了照护与纵容。宠溺一个人可以维持她的依赖：礼物与安慰取代倾听、诚实与承认。这句话也说明，表面的特权并不会取消孤独。

## “Sans toi” and the Voice That Becomes Too Real
《没有你》与变得过于真实的声音

The rehearsal of “Sans toi,” written by Varda with music by Michel Legrand, forms the film's central break. What begins as casual joking becomes a concentrated musical performance. The camera moves closer, the room seems to fall away, and Cléo's stylised role as a singer suddenly carries the fear of abandonment, bodily decay, and death.

由瓦尔达作词、米歇尔·勒格朗作曲的《没有你》排练段落，构成影片的中心转折。最初轻松的玩笑逐渐变成一次高度集中的音乐表演。摄影机向克莱奥靠近，房间仿佛从她周围消失，而她作为歌手的程式化角色突然承载起被遗弃、身体衰败与死亡的恐惧。

The song does not simply express feelings that were already stable inside her. Performance produces an experience she can no longer keep at a decorative distance. When the accompaniment stops and ordinary space returns, Cléo is angry at having been made to sing her fear as though it were entertainment.

这首歌并不只是表达已经稳定存在于她内心的感受。表演制造出一种她再也无法保持装饰性距离的经验。当伴奏停止、普通空间重新出现时，克莱奥愤怒了，因为她被迫把自己的恐惧唱出来，仿佛那只是供人消费的娱乐。

## Removing the Wig
摘下假发

After the rehearsal, Cléo removes her blonde wig and changes into a simpler black dress. The gesture is visually decisive, but it should not be treated as proof that clothing and femininity are inherently false. The wig belongs to a particular working persona: the marketable singer “Cléo Victoire,” whose name, appearance, songs, and emotions have been arranged by other people.

排练之后，克莱奥摘下金色假发，换上一条更简洁的黑裙。这个动作在视觉上十分明确，但不应被理解成服装与女性气质天然虚假。假发属于一种特定的职业人格：可以被出售的歌手“克莱奥·维克图瓦”；她的名字、外貌、歌曲与情绪都受到他人的安排。

Removing it does not reveal a self outside all performance. It creates room for another mode of moving through the city. Cléo leaves behind her entourage and begins to walk without requiring each place to return her own image.

摘下假发并没有显露一个位于所有表演之外的自我。它只是为另一种穿过城市的方式创造空间。克莱奥离开随行者，开始独自行走，不再要求每一个地点都把她自己的形象返还给她。

## A Woman Walking Through Paris
一个女人穿过巴黎

Cléo's walk has often been read through the figure of the *flâneuse*: a woman who claims the urban mobility and observational freedom historically associated with the male *flâneur*. Janice Mouton argues that Cléo's movement through Paris transforms feminine masquerade into a more active practice of looking. [“From Feminine Masquerade to Flâneuse”](https://doi.org/10.1353/cj.2001.0004)

克莱奥的行走常常通过 *flâneuse*（女性漫游者）这一形象得到解释：一个女人取得了历史上通常与男性漫游者相连的城市流动与观察自由。Janice Mouton 指出，克莱奥穿过巴黎的运动，使女性化的假面表演逐渐转化成一种更主动的观看实践。[《从女性假面到女性漫游者》](https://doi.org/10.1353/cj.2001.0004)

Yet the street is not automatically liberating. Cléo remains exposed to comments, stares, noise, spectacle, and the commercial organisation of the city. Her walk matters because it lets her encounter these forces directly rather than only through the protected interiors of the boutique, taxi, apartment, and café.

但街道不会自动带来解放。克莱奥仍然暴露在评论、凝视、噪音、奇观与城市商业秩序之中。她的行走之所以重要，是因为它让她直接遭遇这些力量，而不再只通过精品店、出租车、公寓与咖啡馆等受保护的室内空间接触城市。

## Looking Back at the Crowd
回望人群

As Cléo walks alone, Varda gives increasing attention to the people around her. Faces at café tables, workers, street performers, couples, drivers, and passers-by no longer function only as an audience for the singer. The soundtrack also widens: conversations, radio reports, traffic, footsteps, and fragments of public life compete with Cléo's private anxiety.

当克莱奥独自行走时，瓦尔达逐渐把更多注意力交给周围的人。咖啡桌旁的面孔、劳动者、街头表演者、情侣、司机与行人不再只是歌手的观众。声音空间也随之扩大：谈话、广播新闻、交通、脚步与公共生活的碎片开始与克莱奥的私人焦虑并存。

Her change is not that she stops being visible. It is that visibility becomes reciprocal. She sees that other people occupy the same city and carry experiences that do not revolve around her. The world becomes more crowded and less mirror-like.

她的变化并不在于停止被看见，而在于可见性开始具有相互性。她意识到其他人也生活在同一座城市，并承载着并不围绕她展开的经验。世界变得更加拥挤，也不再那么像一面只反射她的镜子。

## Dorothée and the Nude Body
多萝泰与裸体身体

Cléo visits her friend Dorothée, who poses nude for sculpture students. The scene creates a sharp contrast between two ways a woman's body may become an image. Cléo is clothed, famous, and anxious about how her beauty is judged. Dorothée is naked before a room of artists but describes the work without shame; she distinguishes her living body from the forms the students are trying to make.

克莱奥拜访了为雕塑学生担任裸体模特的朋友多萝泰。这一场景把女性身体成为图像的两种方式并置起来。克莱奥穿着衣服、拥有名声，却焦虑自己的美貌如何被判断；多萝泰赤裸地站在一群艺术学习者面前，却并不感到羞耻，她能够区分自己的活身体与学生试图制作的形态。

The contrast does not mean that nudity is automatically free or that fashion is automatically oppressive. It shows that objectification depends on a relation: who controls the situation, how a body is framed, whether the person can speak, and whether looking allows reciprocity. Emma Wilson's work on Cléo and Dorothée is especially attentive to the ethical and affective complexity of this encounter. [*Agnès Varda Unlimited*, chapter 1](https://doi.org/10.2307/j.ctv16kkxz1.5)

这种对比并不意味着裸体天然自由，也不意味着时尚天然压迫。它说明客体化取决于一种关系：谁控制情境，身体如何被构图，当事人能否发言，以及观看是否允许相互回应。Emma Wilson 关于克莱奥与多萝泰的研究，尤其重视这次相遇在伦理与情感上的复杂性。[《无限的阿涅斯·瓦尔达》第一章](https://doi.org/10.2307/j.ctv16kkxz1.5)

## Illness and the Right to Know
疾病与知情的权利

Cléo's fear is sometimes described as narcissistic because she initially worries about losing beauty. But the film gradually reveals a more serious problem: she does not know what is happening to her body, and almost everyone around her prefers reassurance to truth. Her medical uncertainty exposes a gap between being the person who is ill and possessing authority over the meaning of illness.

克莱奥的恐惧有时被描述成自恋，因为她最初担心失去美貌。但影片逐渐显露出一个更严肃的问题：她并不知道自己的身体正在发生什么，而周围几乎所有人都更愿意提供安慰，而不是事实。她的医学不确定性暴露出一种裂缝：一个人是患病者，却不一定拥有解释疾病的权威。

The real-time structure keeps the viewer inside this asymmetry. We do not receive a privileged diagnosis before Cléo does. We wait with her, collecting the same incomplete signs, and experience how medical time is lived by someone whose future appears to depend on an absent professional answer.

实时结构让观众停留在这种不对称之中。我们并没有比克莱奥更早获得一个特权性的诊断；我们与她一起等待，收集同样不完整的征兆，并体验医学时间如何被一个未来仿佛取决于缺席专业答案的人度过。

## The Algerian War Inside an Intimate Story
亲密故事中的阿尔及利亚战争

The film takes place on 21 June 1961, during the final phase of the Algerian War. News reports are heard on the radio, and Antoine, the young soldier Cléo meets in Parc Montsouris, is due to return to military service in Algeria. The war is not a distant historical footnote. It enters the film through broadcast sound, masculine conscription, public anxiety, and the prospect of death beyond Paris.

影片发生在1961年6月21日，正值阿尔及利亚战争的最后阶段。广播中传来相关新闻，而克莱奥在蒙苏里公园遇见的年轻士兵安托万，即将返回阿尔及利亚服役。战争并不是遥远的历史脚注；它通过广播声音、男性征兵、公共焦虑与巴黎之外的死亡可能性进入影片。

Antoine and Cléo meet as two people living under different threats. Her danger is medical and located inside the body; his is military and located in a colonial war. The film does not declare these experiences equal, but their conversation allows private fear to meet public history. Scholarship on gender and postcolonial space warns against separating Cléo's awakening from the political world through which she walks. [Matthew Sharpe, *Gender and Space in Post-colonial French and Algerian Cinema*](http://hdl.handle.net/10443/2744)

安托万与克莱奥作为承受不同威胁的两个人相遇。她的危险来自医学，位于身体内部；他的危险来自军事，位于一场殖民战争之中。影片没有宣称两种经验完全相同，但他们的谈话让私人恐惧与公共历史发生接触。关于性别与后殖民空间的研究提醒我们，不能把克莱奥的觉醒与她所穿行的政治世界分开。[Matthew Sharpe，《后殖民法国与阿尔及利亚电影中的性别与空间》](http://hdl.handle.net/10443/2744)

## Antoine Is Not a Cure
安托万不是解药

The final encounter is sometimes described as though a sensitive man rescues Cléo from narcissism. That reading gives Antoine too much authorship over her change. Cléo has already removed the wig, rejected the rehearsal, walked alone, visited Dorothée, and begun looking outward before she meets him.

结尾的相遇有时被描述成一个善解人意的男人把克莱奥从自恋中拯救出来。这种解释赋予安托万过多的作者权。克莱奥在遇见他之前，已经摘下假发、拒绝继续排练、独自行走、拜访多萝泰，并开始向外观看。

What Antoine offers is not a cure but reciprocity. He speaks about his own fear and listens to hers. They move side by side rather than arranging themselves as star and admirer. Their brief intimacy matters because neither person needs to pretend that death is absent.

安托万提供的不是解药，而是相互回应。他谈论自己的恐惧，也倾听她的恐惧。两人并肩移动，而不是以明星与仰慕者的关系排列自己。这段短暂亲密之所以重要，是因为任何一方都不再需要假装死亡并不存在。

## The Diagnosis Does Not Restore Certainty
诊断并没有恢复全部确定性

At the hospital, Cléo finally encounters her doctor, who gives her a provisional diagnosis and proposes treatment. The answer is serious but not identical to the catastrophe she has imagined. Medical information narrows uncertainty, yet it does not return her to the person she was at five o'clock.

在医院，克莱奥终于遇见医生。医生给出初步诊断，并提出治疗方案。答案十分严肃，却并不等同于她此前想象的灾难。医学信息缩小了不确定性，但它无法让她重新变回下午五点钟的那个人。

The ending is therefore neither a miraculous cure nor a confirmation of death. Cléo says that she is no longer afraid and seems happy in the present moment. This calm is fragile and specific. It comes not from mastering the future but from having endured the waiting without remaining enclosed inside her own image.

因此，结尾既不是奇迹般的治愈，也不是死亡的确认。克莱奥说自己不再害怕，并在当下显得平静而快乐。这种平静脆弱而具体；它并非来自掌握未来，而是来自她已经度过等待，并且不再继续封闭在自己的形象之中。

## Cinécriture: Writing with Time, Sound, and Place
电影书写：用时间、声音与地点写作

Varda used the term *cinécriture* to describe filmmaking as a form of writing carried out through all the materials of cinema. In *Cléo from 5 to 7*, meaning is not produced by dialogue alone. It emerges through duration, routes, chapter titles, reflections, costumes, street encounters, musical transitions, ambient sound, and the changing distance between camera and body.

瓦尔达使用 *cinécriture*（电影书写）这个词，把电影制作理解成一种运用全部电影材料进行的写作。在《五至七时的克莱奥》中，意义并不只由对白产生。它来自时长、路线、章节标题、倒影、服装、街头相遇、音乐转换、环境声音，以及摄影机与身体之间不断改变的距离。

This approach also troubles the boundary between fiction and documentary. Cléo is a fictional singer, but the streets, crowds, traffic, cafés, construction sites, radio news, and seasonal light preserve Paris at a specific historical moment. Varda does not use the city merely as background; she lets material reality interrupt and enlarge the character's story.

这种方法也扰动了虚构与纪录之间的边界。克莱奥是一位虚构歌手，但街道、人群、交通、咖啡馆、建筑工地、广播新闻与季节光线保存了一个特定历史时刻的巴黎。瓦尔达并没有把城市仅仅当作背景，而是让物质现实打断并扩大人物的故事。

## A Feminist Film Before a Fixed Formula
一部先于固定公式的女性主义电影

The film is frequently described as feminist because it examines how a woman's subjectivity is organised through appearance, work, space, medicine, and looking. Yet it was made before the major feminist film-theory debates of the 1970s. Its importance does not depend on perfectly illustrating a later theory of the male gaze.

这部影片经常被称为女性主义电影，因为它考察女性主体如何通过外貌、劳动、空间、医学与观看被组织起来。但影片创作于1970年代女性主义电影理论重要争论之前，其意义并不依赖于完美证明后来形成的“男性凝视”理论。

Varda's film is more exploratory. Cléo is at once object and observer, privileged and constrained, performing and sincere, frightened and curious. The city offers movement but also surveillance; beauty offers power but also reduction; intimacy offers comfort but not a final answer. The film's feminism lies partly in its refusal to resolve these contradictions into a single exemplary transformation.

瓦尔达的影片更像一项探索。克莱奥既是客体也是观察者，既拥有特权也受到限制，既在表演也有真诚，既恐惧也好奇。城市提供流动，也带来监视；美貌提供力量，也造成缩减；亲密关系给予安慰，却不提供最终答案。影片的女性主义部分存在于它拒绝把这些矛盾化解成一次标准化的成长。

## What Changes Between Five and Half Past Six?
从五点到六点半，究竟改变了什么？

Cléo does not become a completely new woman. She does not cease to be a singer, become indifferent to beauty, conquer illness, or escape dependence forever. A more modest and more radical change occurs: she no longer treats her image as the only proof that she exists.

克莱奥没有成为一个全新的女人。她没有停止做歌手，没有从此对美貌毫不在意，没有战胜疾病，也没有永远摆脱依赖。发生的是一种更有限、也更根本的变化：她不再把自己的形象当作存在的唯一证明。

Once she looks outward, time also changes. Waiting is no longer only a corridor leading toward the doctor's verdict. It contains a city, a friend, a song, a war, a stranger, and the possibility of speaking honestly. The future remains uncertain, but the present is no longer empty.

当她开始向外观看，时间也随之改变。等待不再只是一条通向医生裁决的走廊；它容纳了一座城市、一位朋友、一首歌、一场战争、一个陌生人，以及诚实说话的可能性。未来仍然不确定，但现在不再空无一物。

## Quotation｜引文

> “Everybody spoils me; nobody loves me.”

> “每个人都宠我，却没有人爱我。”

— Cléo, *Cléo from 5 to 7*

This line identifies the emotional economy surrounding Cléo. To spoil her is to keep giving her the signs of special treatment while avoiding the difficult work of listening. She receives attention as an image and indulgence as a dependent woman, but little recognition as a person capable of fear, judgment, and truth.

这句话点出了围绕克莱奥运作的情感经济。宠她，意味着不断给予特殊待遇的标志，却回避倾听这种困难劳动。她作为形象获得注意，作为依赖性的女人获得纵容，却很少作为一个能够感受恐惧、作出判断并面对事实的人得到承认。

> “Today everything amazes me. People's faces next to mine.”

> “今天，一切都让我惊讶。别人的脸，就在我的脸旁边。”

— Cléo, *Cléo from 5 to 7*

The second line marks a change in attention. Cléo's face is no longer isolated as the centre of every frame or reflected back as the measure of reality. It appears beside other faces. Her subjectivity expands not by becoming sovereign over the city, but by recognising proximity and coexistence.

第二句话标记了注意力的变化。克莱奥的脸不再作为每个画面的唯一中心被隔离，也不再作为现实尺度被镜面返还；它出现在其他面孔旁边。她的主体性并非通过成为城市的主宰而扩大，而是通过承认邻近与共存发生改变。

## Scholarship｜学术文献

### Sandy Flitterman-Lewis, “From Déesse to Idée: Cléo from 5 to 7”

In *To Desire Differently: Feminism and the French Cinema*, expanded edition, Columbia University Press, 1996.

Flitterman-Lewis's influential feminist reading examines Cléo's movement from a woman organised as spectacle toward a more reflective subject. The chapter is foundational for discussions of mirrors, masquerade, looking, and the film's challenge to dominant cinematic representations of women.

Flitterman-Lewis 具有影响力的女性主义解读，考察克莱奥如何从一个被组织成奇观的女人，逐渐转向更具反思能力的主体。这一章节是讨论镜子、假面、观看，以及影片如何挑战主导性女性影像的重要基础。

### Janice Mouton, “From Feminine Masquerade to Flâneuse: Agnès Varda's Cléo in the City”

*Cinema Journal*, vol. 40, no. 2, 2001, pp. 3–16. DOI: [10.1353/cj.2001.0004](https://doi.org/10.1353/cj.2001.0004).

Mouton focuses on Cléo's urban movement and develops the figure of the *flâneuse*. Her essay supports reading the walk through Paris as a change in visual agency while keeping the gendered conditions of public space visible.

Mouton 聚焦克莱奥的城市移动，并发展出女性漫游者这一分析形象。她的文章支持把穿过巴黎的行走理解成观看能动性的变化，同时仍然让公共空间的性别条件保持可见。

### Jill Forbes, “Gender and Space in Cléo de 5 à 7”

*Studies in French Cinema*, vol. 2, no. 2, 2002, pp. 83–89. DOI: [10.1386/sfci.2.2.83](https://doi.org/10.1386/sfci.2.2.83).

Forbes revisits canonical feminist interpretations that describe Cléo as an empowered female subject. By examining the film's mapping of Cléo onto traditions of imagining Paris as woman, she shows why the relation between movement, space, and objectification remains ambiguous.

Forbes 重新审视了把克莱奥描述成获得赋权的女性主体的经典女性主义解释。她分析影片如何把克莱奥放进“把巴黎想象成女人”的传统，由此说明移动、空间与客体化之间的关系为何始终具有歧义。

### Elizabeth Ezra, “Cléo's Masks: Regimes of Objectification in the French New Wave”

*Yale French Studies*, nos. 118/119, 2010, pp. 177–190. [JSTOR 41337086](https://www.jstor.org/stable/41337086).

Ezra begins with the masks Cléo notices through a taxi window and connects masquerade to wider histories of cultural and sexual commodification. Her essay broadens objectification beyond gender alone and situates the film's visual objects within histories of colonial expropriation and violence.

Ezra 从克莱奥透过出租车窗看到的面具出发，把假面与文化、性商品化的更广泛历史联系起来。她的文章把客体化扩展到性别之外，并把影片中的视觉物件放进殖民掠夺与暴力的历史中理解。

### Claudia Gorbman, “Cléo from Five to Seven: Music as Mirror”

*Wide Angle*, vol. 4, no. 4, 1981, pp. 38–49.

Gorbman's analysis treats music as a structural mirror within the film. It is especially relevant to the relation between the chaptered clock, Cléo's recorded songs, street sound, and the central performance of “Sans toi.”

Gorbman 的分析把音乐理解成影片内部的一面结构性镜子。这篇文章尤其有助于理解章节化时钟、克莱奥的录制歌曲、街道声音与核心表演《没有你》之间的关系。

### Wenxin Liang, “Cinécriture Féminine: Female Subjectivity in Varda's Cléo from 5 to 7 (1962) in Comparison with Godard's Films”

*Film Matters*, vol. 15, no. 1, 2024, pp. 7–30. DOI: [10.1386/fm_00319_1](https://doi.org/10.1386/fm_00319_1).

Liang compares Varda's construction of female subjectivity with the treatment of women in Godard's films of the same period. The article is useful for locating Varda's *cinécriture* within the male-dominated history of the French New Wave.

Liang 把瓦尔达对女性主体的建构，与戈达尔同时期电影中的女性呈现进行比较。这篇文章有助于把瓦尔达的电影书写放进男性占主导地位的法国新浪潮历史中定位。
